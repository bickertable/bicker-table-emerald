From Using AI to Operating with AI

I'm an AI Systems Architect for organizations that run on people. I help established schools and service businesses in the US and UK move from staff using AI on their own to AI that works across the operation with people directing it.

The thesis

Individual AI productivity is not organizational intelligence.

Staff get faster on their own. Proposals, notes, and client updates move. Who owns the work, how it is handed off, and what a new person learns on day one can stay exactly as they were. Widespread use is a stage. Shared direction is the next one.

Who I work with

Organizations that run on people.

Two kinds of leaders call me. Both already have people using AI. Both are ready to decide where it should work across the operation.

Service businesses

Owners and CEOs

Established firms in the US and UK, about 20 to 250 people. Consulting, professional services, home-based care, real estate, and other businesses where the work moves through people.

For businesses

Schools

Heads and trust leaders

Heads of independent, private, and charter schools, and leaders of UK academy trusts. The question is how staff, operations, and families meet AI with the school still directing it.

For schools

A scene you may recognize

Faster at the desk. The same operation in the hallway.

Using AI

Picture a firm of about 60 people. Nearly every desk has a chat window open. Proposals take less time. Someone in operations built a private way to draft client reports. Two people in business development keep prompt notes in their own accounts. By the look of the tools, the firm is moving.

Operating with AI

Picture an ordinary Wednesday after leadership treats AI as an operating question. A new hire opens a short guide: which tools are approved, what client information stays out of them, and which prompts the team has tested. When a workflow gets better, the firm keeps it. When a client asks how their information is handled, there is an answer, because someone decided.

The first picture is common, and it is useful. The second is a decision. I help you make that decision from where you actually are.

How I work

Investigate. Map. Diagnose. Prioritize. Design. Implement.

This is the sequence I use so a recommendation has to pass through the operation, not only through a tool demo. People stay in charge of it.

01

Investigate

I learn how the organization actually runs: the people, the work, and where attention is going.

02

Map

I trace how work, information, and decisions move today.

03

Diagnose

I look at where AI use is already strong, where it is still personal, and what that makes possible next.

04

Prioritize

We choose the opportunities with the most value for the operation, in an order the team can carry.

05

Design

I shape ownership, shared practice, and the sequence people will use.

06

Implement

We put that structure to work, with people still directing it.
