Opportunity Brief

Start with the call. Go further only if it earns it.

The first step is a 15 to 20 minute call called From Using AI to Operating with AI. If we both see a reason to continue, the Agentic AI Opportunity Brief is the discovery I use. It is a structured look at the operation. It is not a full AI audit, and it is not an implementation plan.

Who it is for

The same two audiences.

Owners and CEOs of established service businesses in the US and UK, about 20 to 250 people. Heads of independent, private, and charter schools, and leaders of UK academy trusts.

What the first call is

In 15 to 20 minutes, we'll look at how your team is using AI today and where it could create the most value next. You'll leave with a clear picture of your starting point and one practical next step. No pitch, no prep needed. Just bring your questions.

What the brief looks at

Fifteen parts. Some of them are the conversation. Some are work I do after we talk, so the time together stays on your operation. I do not ask every question. I follow what you have already told me. Business first. Technology later.

  1. Business snapshot. I learn what the organization actually is before I form an opinion about what it needs.
  2. The commercial outcome. Any AI work has to connect to a result leadership already cares about.
  3. AI ambition and expectations. I separate what people hope AI will do from what the operation needs next.
  4. Value and capacity constraints. I look for where time, attention, or revenue is tight before anyone chooses a tool.
  5. Current workflow discovery. We walk one valuable workflow from the moment it starts to the moment it is done.
  6. Human, agent, and automation potential. We decide what should stay with people, what a tool can carry, and what needs a person to approve.
  7. Context, knowledge, and data. I look at whether the information the work depends on is shared and usable.
  8. Technology and integration environment. I learn which systems are already in place before anyone proposes a new one.
  9. Authority, risk, and exceptions. We agree what AI may recommend, draft, or do, and what always stays with a person.
  10. Measurement and business case. We name a baseline, so a later change has something honest to be compared with.
  11. People, adoption, and ownership. We name who would use it, who sponsors it, and who looks after it.
  12. Rapid opportunity prioritization. After the conversation, I rank where value and readiness meet.
  13. Top agentic AI opportunities. I hold the list to a few. One clear opportunity is more useful than a catalog.
  14. Strategic findings. You get a clear read of the strongest opening, the constraint in the way, and what to leave alone for now.
  15. Recommended path forward. One next step. Sometimes that step is to wait, or to strengthen the operation before any new tool.

You do not need to prepare a deck. If we continue past the first call, it helps to have a sense of the one result you would most like to improve, and one workflow you can describe from memory.

Questions

Before you book

Is this the same as the 15 to 20 minute call?

No. The first conversation is the 15 to 20 minute call, From Using AI to Operating with AI. It is 15 to 20 minutes. The Agentic AI Opportunity Brief is the longer discovery I use when that conversation shows a reason to go further.

What do I leave the first call with?

A clear picture of your starting point and one practical next step. No pitch, and no prep.

Is the brief a technical audit?

No. It is an early-stage look at the operation: where you are, what you are trying to accomplish, and whether AI could create value. It is not an implementation specification.

Who is it for?

Owners and CEOs of established service businesses in the US and UK, about 20 to 250 people, and heads of independent, private, and charter schools and UK academy trusts.

What if the honest next step is to wait?

Then that is the recommendation. A clear decision to wait, or to do foundational work first, is a useful outcome.
