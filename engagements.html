Questions We Get

The questions worth asking before you engage.

These are the questions executives actually ask us, including the uncomfortable ones. We have answered them directly rather than diplomatically.

What this actually is
Why can't I just use AI to write my own articles?

You can, and you should not let anyone tell you otherwise. Writing an article is no longer the hard part.

The article is not the product. The work is deciding what you should become known for, extracting the ideas and frameworks that are uniquely yours, building a connected body of evidence around them, putting that evidence where both people and AI systems can retrieve it, and deliberately getting it in front of the right people.

An AI tool will happily write you twelve competent articles about your industry. None of them will be about you, because the tool has no way to find the pattern in twenty years of your judgment. That extraction is the engagement.

What if I already have a marketing person?

Then you are in a better position than most, and we would rather work with them than around them.

A strong marketing team can distribute and amplify a position. Our work begins one layer earlier: determining what the position should be and extracting the intellectual property that makes it defensible.

The most common outcome is that your marketing people become considerably more effective, because they finally have a defined position and a body of owned thinking to work from rather than deciding what to write about this month.

How is this different from hiring a social media manager?

A social media manager runs channels. This builds intellectual property.

The output of channel management is activity: posts, engagement, follower counts. The output here is an asset. A named framework, a body of connected thinking, and a technical foundation that makes both discoverable. Activity stops the day someone stops posting. Intellectual property keeps working.

They are also not mutually exclusive. Plenty of clients keep their social manager and give them something considerably better to distribute.

Do you use AI to write my content?

Yes, under human direction, and we put that in the agreement rather than leaving it unsaid.

AI accelerates research, analysis, and drafting. It does not decide what you should be known for, and nothing reaches you without human editorial review. The judgment is ours, the expertise is yours, and the tooling is what makes the timeline possible at this price.

Your time and involvement
How much of my time will this take?

Less than you expect, but not zero, and anyone promising zero is selling you something that will not work.

Expect discovery and interview sessions early, then review and approval as work comes back to you. You do no building. No writing, no schema, no publishing, no technical setup. You show up and think out loud, and we do everything after that.

Authority IP is extracted from your expertise. There is no version of this where we build it without you in the room.

Does this mean I have to post on LinkedIn all the time?

No, and consistency matters far more than volume.

What we build is a distribution approach sized to how you actually work, not a posting quota. A smaller number of substantial pieces that demonstrate real thinking outperforms daily activity for the kind of buyer you are trying to reach.

Who does the posting depends on the engagement level. At the foundational level we build the strategy and architecture and you or your team execute it. At the managed level we operate it for you, including scheduling, publishing, and repurposing.

What if I don't want to become a content creator?

Good. That is not what this is, and if it were we would be building the wrong thing for you.

A content creator's business is attention. Yours is judgment. What we are building is the documented, retrievable version of expertise you already have, aimed at a small number of people who need exactly what you do. That is closer to publishing a body of professional work than to running a channel.

The measure of success is not audience size. It is whether the right person finds the right thinking at the moment they are deciding.

Results and expectations
Are you guaranteeing leads?

This is not traditional lead generation, and we will not promise you a number of clients we do not control. It is designed to create more opportunities for the right people to discover, understand and engage with your expertise.

We build the authority and then deliberately put it into circulation: search, AI retrieval, publication, distribution, LinkedIn, and strategic network development. That is active work, not passive waiting.

What we do not control is your pricing, your sales conversations, your response time, or whether a given prospect is a fit. Anyone promising a specific number of clients from an authority engagement is either guessing or quietly counting something else. We guarantee our side of the work, specifically and in writing.

How quickly will this show up in the business?

Three horizons, and we would rather set them honestly than oversell the first one.

During the engagement. The positioning becomes clear and the first usable authority assets begin appearing. You start having sharper conversations because you can finally articulate what you do in a way that is specific rather than general.

Over the following months. The published evidence has time to circulate, index, be retrieved, and support business development. This is where we measure against the baseline we established before any work began.

Over time. A sufficiently strong body of work can influence shortlist position, referral credibility, pricing power, and category association. We are deliberate about the word can. Those are outcomes we are working toward, not results we control.

Why does AI visibility matter if most of my business comes from referrals?

Referrals are the strongest business development there is, and nothing here replaces them.

But a referral depends on someone else remembering to put your name in the room. You have no control over whether that happens, and no visibility into the times it does not.

Authority infrastructure does two things for that model. It gives referrals somewhere to land, so when someone hears your name and looks you up, what they find confirms the recommendation instead of raising a question. And it creates additional paths for people to discover and validate you when no referral happens at all.

This strengthens the way you already win work and adds a second way alongside it.

Do you help me get this in front of the right people?

Yes. Publishing is only part of the system.

Once there is enough Authority IP in market to support the position, we begin identifying the people and professional relationships who should know your work, and develop appropriate outreach to help initiate those relationships. The depth of that work depends on the engagement you select.

We do not believe in sending people toward an empty shelf. Reaching out before there is substance behind your name wastes the introduction, which is why this comes later in the sequence rather than first.

What we do not do is have your relationships for you. Introductions, conversations, and the judgment about who is worth your time remain yours. What changes is that the people you meet have already encountered your thinking, so those conversations start further along.

Ownership and what comes next
What happens after the six weeks?

You own everything built, and you have a foundation that does not expire.

Six weeks is enough to establish the position, name the framework, and produce the cornerstone body of work. It is not enough to compound it. Compounding is what the longer engagements are for, and around week four you will have a clear view of whether extending makes sense. That decision point is built into the structure rather than bolted on as a sales conversation at the end.

Plenty of clients take the foundation and run it themselves from there. That is a legitimate outcome, not a failure.

What happens to everything we create after the engagement?

It is yours, and it is not disposable marketing inventory.

Authority IP compounds. Campaign content expires. The cornerstone pieces keep working as LinkedIn content, newsletter material, presentation and speaking material, sales collateral, proposal language, research foundations, and potentially the architecture of a future book. The same body of work keeps being useful in new forms for years.

Legally, finished deliverables transfer to you on payment. More importantly, any framework, model, or terminology derived from your own expertise belongs to you outright, whether or not we were the ones who named it, structured it, or first wrote it down.

What we retain is our own delivery methodology, meaning the systems and processes we use to do this work across clients. You are not buying our machinery, and we are not claiming your intellectual property.

Why do I need a separate personal website if my company already has one?

Because they answer two different questions, and one of them is about you.

Your company site sells services. It tells a buyer what the firm delivers and how to engage it. A personal authority property holds the thinking: the frameworks, the perspective, the body of work that establishes why your judgment is worth paying for. AI systems and search engines treat a person and an organization as distinct entities, and expertise attributed to a firm does not automatically attach to you.

There is also a durability argument. Firms get renamed, restructured, merged, and sold. Authority attached to you personally travels with you. Authority attached only to a company name does not.

Could this eventually become a book or a speaking platform?

It can, and a coherent framework is the prerequisite for both. Most people attempt these in the wrong order.

Speaking and publishing both require a defined idea you are known for. Without one, a book proposal is a collection of chapters and a speaker pitch is a list of jobs. The work here produces that defined idea first, which is what makes either path viable afterward.

Whether we develop those specifically depends on the engagement. Longer engagements include work on speaking territories and book architecture where the material warrants it. Neither promises a publishing deal or booked engagements, because those depend on editors, committees, and organizers we do not control.

What happens to my confidential client information?

A mutual non-disclosure agreement is signed before discovery begins, not after.

Where your material includes information belonging to your own clients or their people, we ask you to redact or de-identify it before it reaches us, and we do not request identifiable personal data we do not need. Anyone on our side with access is bound by the same obligations we are.

The question we cannot answer from the outside: whether your expertise is strong enough to be worth documenting. The problem is rarely a shortage of expertise. It is that decades of judgment have never been turned into evidence that people, search engines and AI systems can find, understand and retrieve.
