For businesses

Your team is already using AI. The firm can direct what happens next.

I work with owners and CEOs of established service businesses in the US and UK, about 20 to 250 people. The work is to move from staff using AI on their own to AI that works across the operation, with people directing it.

What it looks like now

A collection of faster people is not yet a smarter firm.

The manual nobody wrote

The person who experiments over lunch has a prompt library, a way through intake notes, and a reporting shortcut. It lives in a personal account. You did not appoint them. When they leave, that learning can leave with them. The issue is not that people are experimenting. It is that the firm's best thinking about AI is still private.

One plan for every team

Marketing may already draft with AI. Finance may still live in the spreadsheet. Operations may have started and stopped. A single rollout, bought for the keenest team, often shines there and stalls everywhere else. Each function needs its own next step.

The question from outside

A client asks how their information is handled when AI tools are in the work. An insurer asks about data practices. A partner asks to see the firm's approach before signing. Those moments go better when someone has already decided, and the answer belongs to the firm.

When it is working

New hires see which tools are approved and what stays out of them. A reporting workflow is documented and shared. Business development updates a prompt, and the library everyone uses changes with it. The best thinking compounds. People still direct the work.

How this applies

One function, one next step.

I do not start by picking a platform for the whole firm. I start with where each part of the business actually is. Sometimes the next step is a shared prompt library. Sometimes it is a conversation about how a client request moves, before anyone buys another tool. Sometimes a function waits a quarter, on purpose.

The sequence is the same one I use everywhere: investigate, map, diagnose, prioritize, design, implement. You can read how the longer discovery is structured on the Opportunity Brief page. The first conversation is shorter than that.

The Tuesday essays are written for this audience. Week 1 is the undocumented operating manual. Week 2 is the different starting point behind every door.
