For schools

Your staff are already using AI. The school can decide what that becomes.

I work with heads of independent, private, and charter schools, and with leaders of UK academy trusts. I spent 10 years teaching, including K-12, adults, and Title I middle grades. The work now is to help a school move from individual use to a way of operating that people still direct.

What it looks like on a school day

A staff room can be several starting points at once.

The classroom and the front office

Picture a teacher who drafts a family letter with AI before first period, and an admissions desk that still rebuilds the same answers for every tour. Both are doing the job in front of them. The school has not yet chosen which of those habits should become the school's way.

The new teacher

Department chairs can hold different expectations for student work and AI. A new teacher learns them by asking around. A shared, short guide does not flatten professional judgment. It tells people what the school has actually decided.

The board question

A trustee or governor asks what the school's approach to AI is. A useful answer is specific: where staff are using it, what student information stays out, who owns the next step, and what the school is not doing yet.

Several schools, one trust

In a group of schools, each staff room can have its own habits. One site is not the average of the trust. Naming the starting point in each school, or each function, is what makes the next step practical.

Your school chooses its own starting point. I help you see where AI can create value next, with teachers and leaders still directing it. Families, governance, and the people who do the work stay in the picture.

The closest verified US figure

33.4% of US firms in the Educational sector used AI in a recent two-week window.

Read that carefully. It is not a count of schools, teachers, or classrooms. It is not a UK figure, and I do not have a verified UK academy-trust statistic to set beside it. It is the U.S. Census Bureau's Educational sector: the share of those firms that used AI in any business function in the last two weeks.

How I work with a school

Same sequence. School language.

01

Investigate

I learn the school: students and families, staff, operations, and what leadership is paying attention to this term.

02

Map

I trace how a real piece of work moves. Admissions questions, family communication, teacher planning, or reporting are usual places to start. One workflow, not the whole school at once.

03

Diagnose

I look at where use is already strong, where it is still personal, and what information should stay with people.

04

Prioritize

We choose one next step the staff can carry, and we name what can wait.

05

Design

I help shape ownership, a short shared practice, and what governance needs to see.

06

Implement

The school puts that to work. People direct it. I do not hand the judgment to a tool.
